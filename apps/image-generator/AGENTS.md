# Image Generator — sub-application

The Image Generator edits a gallery of layered images per tab: a filmstrip of images to add, open, duplicate, rename, delete, and reorder, and on the image shown brush and eraser with pen pressure, the clone stamp and the healing brush, rectangle and lasso selections, move, moving selected pixels, crop, eyedropper, text layers, masks, Curves and Color Grade adjustment layers, layers and groups with blend modes, opacity, fill, and locks, layers linked to the files they were made from, notes, the pre-vis check, undo, autosave in the project, PNG and JPEG in and out, and WebP in; and the Prompt page's cell, the rounds of takes its requests made, the round views, a next round from a take, layers written out as PNG, publishing an edit of a take with its canon seal, and previews of images for the host. The host draws the shown image and the filmstrip as a `raster` tab, owns the camera and the pointer, and sends each finished gesture, the keyboard while a text is typed in, and each click on the strip. The contracts are Spumcake Studio 0.10's, 0.11's, and 0.13's `systems.md` in the team repository, milestones 1 to 3 of the Image and Video build plan, the lifecycle's `request-page.md`, and the Studio-as-a-setup contract.

## Rules for this sub-application

- It is its own program. Its crate depends on `protocol`, `serde`, `serde_json`, `image` (PNG and JPEG, and WebP, which the owner approved on 2026-11-16), `ab_glyph` (pinned at 0.2.32, for text layers, as the owner approved on 2026-10-31), and `layers`, what it shares with the Video Generator (linked files, notes, the pre-vis check's edges, round layouts, and the views' labels in Inter, as Studio 0.15's plan approved), and nothing else, never on the host or on another sub-application.
- Text is drawn only from the fonts the app ships, built into the program with `include_bytes!`: Inter's roman variable file, which ships in `layers/assets/fonts/` and is read as `layers::label::INTER`, and, in this crate's `assets/fonts/` with Inter's licence beside them, Inter's italic variable file and Hack, egui's monospace, whose bold and italic are drawn by thickening and slanting it; Inter is bold through its weight axis. Text layers, pins' cards, and the round views' wrapped captions use `src/text.rs`; the round views' one-line labels, the takes' numbers, and the pins' numbers use `layers::label`. Never the computer's fonts: a future web version must find every font in the app.
- Everything the host shows for it is declared through the protocol: its tabs, toolbar and settings row, menu, keys, the Layers and Prompt pages, the filmstrip with its rounds, and the raster contents. Nothing about it is hard-coded in the host.
- A take changes nothing until the assistant puts it in the chain. The workspace keeps editing state only: its `.ipx` holds a cell's step pointer or a draft, never a take, a prompt sent, a run, or a record of what made anything. Rounds and takes are read from what the host sends with `takes`, every time.
- It owns every image of a gallery, and every pixel, layer, selection, crop box, step of history, and file. The host only draws the image and thumbnails this program writes.
- Each image keeps its own history, and Undo acts on the image shown. The filmstrip's changes (add, duplicate, remove, rename, reorder) are steps of the strip's own history, which Undo acts on after the host sends the strip's `focus`, until choosing an image on the strip, a gesture, a panel edit, or a command on the image gives the keyboard back. A thumbnail click is `focus` then `select`, so Undo after it is the shown image's.
- It runs with no host, driven only through its standard input and standard output.
- It speaks the protocol defined in `protocol/` and nothing else. When it needs something the protocol cannot say, change the protocol, the host, and the scaffolding skill together. Do not work around the protocol.
- `manifest.json` registers it with the host. Its id, folder, crate, and program names are the same.

## Layout

- `src/layered.rs` — the image: the tree of layers and groups (settings only), each node's kind (pixels, text, Curves, or Color Grade), mask, and link, each layer's and mask's pixels by id, the blend modes, the locks, and the composite, with each feathered mask blurred once and each text drawn once until it changes. Curves use `draw::ink`'s monotone cubic, copied line for line, so the host's drawn curve is the one applied; a curve's tangents are worked out once, and each adjustment's tables are made once for a composite and kept by their settings. The composite is made in bands of rows, one per core, each a few rows at a time so they stay in the core's cache, with a spare buffer for each depth of groups rather than fresh memory, and can start from the image as the nodes beneath one node combine it (`Beneath`): the top-level nodes beneath it and, for a node within groups, its siblings beneath it within each group, by themselves. That gives the same bytes as combining every node. A feathered mask is blurred by one thread per core, taking a band of rows, then a band of columns, in turn, each line summed in the same order as a line at a time (`blur_alone`), so the values are the same to the bit, into memory kept from the last blur.
- `src/text.rs` — a text's words, style, and box: its layout, following the storyboard's rules in `draw/src/text.rs`, drawn with ab_glyph; the caret, the selection's spans, and the character under a point.
- `src/paint.rs` — the brush and eraser's coverage, which skips pixels already fully covered, and how a stroke changes pixels; what the clone stamp copies and what the healing brush paints (`Healer`), its two sums made side by side in bands of columns, only over the rows its averages read, and its colours worked out as they are painted, in bands of rows, each band taken by the next free thread; its bytes match healing on one thread (`heal_colours_alone`) within one level, as a test checks with the reused memory full of a stroke's leftovers; the selection's coverage mask, its four ways of joining, feather, and outline.
- `src/doc.rs` — one image as it is edited: every change a person or the assistant makes, each one undo step that keeps only the region that changed, and the composite brought up to date only where it changed. It holds the stroke being drawn, the selected pixels being moved, and the text being typed in, and the image's notes, the mark being drawn, a pin's words being typed in, and its pre-vis check. A value a layer already has changes nothing and is no edit. Its `generation` changes whenever pixels may have changed, and not for a change to the tree's settings alone, so what was made from the pixels holds while it stays; a mask blurred or a text drawn again from its settings does not change it either. What holds: the image beneath the layer a slider changes, so each value combines only that layer and what lies above it. It is made while the program is idle for the active layer (`keep_ahead`), so the first held value is as quick as the rest, and kept while its nodes and the pixels are as they were; one made for a slider that began before it was ready is let go with the slider. A group's adjustment keeps the group's own layers beneath it too, and a group's opacity all of its layers; the bottom layer has nothing beneath it, so its sliders combine every layer; a Curves layer's histograms, of what lies beneath it, so dragging the curve never makes them again; and a file's copy of the layers.
- `src/notes.rs` — drawing an image's notes: the pins' discs and marks through `layers::notes`, the numbers through `layers::label`, an open pin's card in the app's fonts, growing with its words up to 320 pixels wide and then wrapping them, kept inside the image, the notes laid over the view, and the marked image.
- `src/previs.rs` — the pre-vis check's settings, its render, and drawing it over any part of the view as `layers::edges` draws it over the whole.
- `src/gallery.rs` — one tab's gallery: its images in order, the one shown, its cells and the one the Prompt page shows, and the filmstrip's history, which holds a removed image, with its own history, until it is put back, and the cells' changes.
- `src/rounds.rs` — a tab's rounds as `takes` sends them: the strip's tiles, each take read from its file, its fingerprint and thumbnail; the take shown and its frame; and the round views, drawn with `layers::layout` and the app's fonts.
- `src/session.rs` — the tabs, the protocol messages, the filmstrip's commands and which of strip or image has the keyboard, files and autosave, and the images and thumbnails written for the host.
- `src/session/requests.rs` — the cells' messages, `takes`, showing a take, a take's menu, a layer written out, `render`'s answer, and `preview`.
- `src/session/filing.rs` — the marked image a next round sends, written in the project's `work/`, filed with `file_resource`, and put in the draft when `filed` names it.
- `src/session/publish.rs` — publishing: the artifact an image publishes as, whether it is an edit, the refusal while a take beneath it is not canon, `publish` and `published`, and the canon seal's `canon_history`, `history_open`, and `history_revert`.
- `src/declare.rs` — the toolbar and settings row as the approved views show them, the pre-vis check's button and chevron, the round views' buttons, the panel's Layers, Properties, Adjustments, and Prompt pages with the Notes row and linked layers' marks, the filmstrip with its rounds and a take's menu, the menu, the keys, and the tab list.
- `src/assist.rs` — the assistant's "Image Generator" tool group: the gallery's tools, `look` at any image, and the shown image's layers, texts, masks, and adjustments. Painting, cloning, healing, and moving pixels stay the person's.
- `src/files.rs` — the `.ipx` file, with linked layers read from their files and a cell kept as a pointer or a draft (`stored_cell`), PNG and JPEG in and out, WebP in, and whole-file writes.

## What it writes for the host

The loop in `main.rs` wakes after 250 ms without a line for `tick`, and after 30 ms when the image beneath the active layer is still to be made, which it then makes before anything else; only the image shown keeps one, as it is the image's size in floating point for each depth of groups; an image no longer shown lets its go at the next idle moment. The host's `shown`, which revision it drew, is dropped as it is read: it follows every image sent, and as a line it would keep the loop from ever being idle.

After every change it sends `raster`, with the filmstrip in `film`. The image shown is the composite with the pre-vis check and the notes drawn over it, when there are any (`Doc::shown`), and is `<tab>-<image>.rgba`, one file for each image of the gallery, in a folder of the session's own in memory, `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/inpainter/image-generator/<process>-<number>/`, when the system has that folder, as the files are written many times a second, read by the host, and never kept; it stays there when a project is named, goes when the program ends, and a folder left by a program no longer running is removed when one starts. Without it, the file is in `<project>/.spumcake/cache/image-generator/`, or a scratch folder under the system's temporary folder with no project. The image is width × height × 4 bytes, RGBA with straight alpha, rows top to bottom, replaced whole by writing `<name>.partial` and renaming it. Each layer's thumbnail is `<tab>-<image>-<layer id>.rgba` beside it, the image's shape fitted into 56 × 40, and each image's filmstrip thumbnail is `<tab>-film-<image>.rgba`, its composite at 44 pixels tall and no more than 176 wide; `<image>` is a number unique within the tab for this run. Each is written again only when its image changed, so an image shown again is the file and revision the host already holds. While a slider is held, the thumbnails and the filmstrip's wait, and are written when it is let go.

A held slider's or curve's values, `property` with `preview`, are applied as they come, in order with everything else, and the image and panel they make are sent once for all the lines waiting, in `main.rs`, as a stroke's parts are; a value that changes nothing sends nothing. Any other message first sends what the values changed, so its answers come after them, except a panel edit or `finish_edit`, which send everything themselves. The value let go, `property` without `preview` or `finish_edit`, is always applied and sent, and the held values and it are one step. A changed image's `patch` names the part that differs from the revision the host was last told, so the host reads only those rows.

A brush or eraser stroke arrives as it is drawn, in `stroke_part` gestures, and `doc.rs` paints each part at once with the finished stroke's brush, without a step of history: each pixel keeps the most coverage any piece gave it, and is painted again from the layer as it was before the stroke. The lines waiting are all read before the image is sent, in `main.rs`. While a stroke is drawn, a view writes only the rows it changed, as shown, with the check and the notes over them (`Doc::shown_rows`), to `<tab>-<image>-<revision>.patch`, named in its `patch.file`, and the last 32 are kept for a host that falls behind. Views say the image is still being made until the program is idle, when `tick` writes the image file whole under the same revision, sends the view without a file, and removes the row files. The finished `stroke` makes the painted stroke one step without painting it again; `stroke_cancel`, or any message that could change the image, takes it off. The host's ping, the camera, and the viewport leave it be. Revisions start from the time the program started and rise with every file written, so an image from an earlier run, or another image of the gallery, is never taken for the current one.

The clone stamp and the healing brush are declared as `sampler`. Alt-click sends `source`; a stroke's offset is from its first point to the source, kept for the next stroke when Aligned is on (healing always is), and declared as `offset` while it holds. Both copy from the layers up to and including the active one as the stroke began, so adjustments above are left out and a stroke never copies itself, and both paint part by part as the brush does. The stamp's release becomes the step as it was painted; healing, `src(p+o) − blur(src)(p+o) + light(p)` with `light` the image's normalised blur leaving out the stroke's own pixels, heals the whole stroke again on release, and that is its step.

With a selection, the Move tool's `drag_part` lifts the selected pixels off the active layer, leaving it clear there, and draws them and the selection moved; `drag` sets them down as one step, and `stroke_cancel` puts them back.

A mask belongs to its node, as `Node.mask`: grey pixels by the mask's own id, at the node's offset, which multiply the node's coverage, white showing and black hiding, outside its pixels showing. It moves, reorders, crops, and is deleted with its node, and has its own row directly above it in the Layers list. Chosen from that row, the brush paints the lightness of its colour into it and the eraser paints black; the stamp and healing do not act on it. An adjustment changes what is already combined beneath it within its group, mixed in by its opacity and its mask; groups keep combining their layers by themselves, so an adjustment inside a group changes only that group.

The T tool's click sends `click` and a drag `rect`: a new text, or the text whose box was clicked, is typed in, and `RasterView.text` carries its box, caret, and selected spans. The keyboard and the pointer inside the box arrive as `raster_text`; Copy and Cut are answered with `clipboard`. Each editing session is one step: a style change, a box drag (`text_box`), or Undo first makes the typing its own step, and Escape, a click outside, another tool, or any other change ends editing. A text left empty is taken away.

## Linked layers

A pixel layer made from a PNG or JPEG outside the image — an image opened as a tab, from the filmstrip's "Open PNG or JPEG…", as new work, or with "Open as layer…" — links to its file as `Node.link`: a `layers::link::Link` of its path, relative to the project's folder when the host has named a project and the file is inside it and whole otherwise, and the sha256 of its bytes. The file is never written. An unedited linked layer keeps no pixels in the `.ipx`, and is read from its file when the image opens: as it is when it is still the one linked or has other bytes (`LinkState::Changed`), and empty, the image's size, when it cannot be read (`Missing`); its link keeps the sha256 it was made with either way, and its row's mark is `link`, or `link-xmark` when missing. A relative path is read from the project's folder, or, with no project, the `.ipx`'s own.

Any step that changes the layer's pixels — painting, erasing, the stamp, healing, moving selected pixels, or a crop — makes it edited, in `Doc::commit`: from then its pixels are kept in the `.ipx` as any layer's, the link says what it was edited from, and its row's mark is `edit-pencil`. The mark is a tree change added to that step, so undoing the step that first changed its pixels makes it unedited again; a later step undone leaves it edited. Moving the whole layer or changing its settings changes no pixels and leaves it unedited. The row's second line, the file's name, "Edited from <file>", or "Missing: <file>", is `declare::row_detail`, sent as `LayerRow.detail`.

## Notes

The notes tool, `chat-bubble`, follows the magnifier and has no key. Its settings row is the marks' colour, red at first (`layers::notes::RED`), and Pin or Draw (`notes_mode`). With Pin, declared as the `text` raster tool, a click puts a pin numbered after the highest (`Notes::next_number`), or on a pin opens it, and its words are typed in as a text's are: `RasterView.text` carries the card's box, and the keyboard arrives as `raster_text`. A click outside, Escape, or any other change ends the typing; the new pin and its words are one step. Backspace or Delete in a pin with no words takes it away, and a drag from a pin moves it, one step. With Draw, declared as a brush of the mark's width, about four pixels on the screen, a drag makes a freehand mark, drawn part by part as it comes, and one step when let go.

Notes are each image's own (`Doc.notes`, a `layers::notes::Notes`), kept apart from its layers: they are drawn over the image shown, the pins' discs and the marks by `layers::notes`, the numbers and the open pin's card with the app's fonts, but never into the composite, an export, a thumbnail, or the file's pixels. While an image has notes, the Layers page shows one Notes row at the top, which is no layer: its eye hides and shows them, not as a step, its click chooses the notes tool, and its menu's Delete takes every note away, one step; a place dropped among the top-level rows counts from below it. `Doc::marked_image`, the composite with every mark and every pin and its number, hidden or not, and `Doc::notes_words` are for a next round, in milestone 2.

## The pre-vis check

The toolbar's cube, "Pre-vis check", turns the check on and off for the image shown; with no render chosen yet, it asks for one through the host's file dialog first, and the render chosen turns it on. Beside it, its chevron's rows choose the render, Edges or See-through, and the see-through opacity. Edges draws the render's edges in `layers::edges::CYAN`, as `layers::edges::check` does, and See-through blends the render at the opacity, as `layers::edges::see_through` does; the render fitted to the image and its edges are made once for each size. Like the notes, it is drawn only over the image shown. Each image keeps its own check, its render named as a link names its file, and read again from that file when the image opens; one that cannot be read draws nothing, and its row says "Missing:".

The filmstrip's actions are `film_focus`, `film_select` (`value`: a position, and `extend` for a Ctrl- or Shift-click), `film_reorder` (`from`, `to`), `film_new`, `film_open`, and each thumbnail's `film_duplicate`, `film_rename`, and `film_delete` (`image`: a position in the gallery). Rename is a row with `rename` set: the host renames in place and sends the name as `value`. A `film_rename` with no `value` tells the person to ask the assistant, since the protocol's questions have no text field.

## The Prompt page

Each tab has cells, kept in its `.ipx` as `cells`, and the one the Prompt page shows, `cell`: each a pointer to a chain step, `step` with nothing else, or a draft before its first run, its own prompt, references, settings, model, and endpoint. A tab with none makes an empty image draft, which is no step, its Size the image's, written "1536 × 864"; a next round's new draft takes its takes' size. Images and Seed are declared as numbers, which the panel draws as sliders: the protocol has no plain number field for a cell's setting yet. They are sent with `cells`, before the panel that names one, whenever they change. The panel's fourth page, Prompt, declares `Properties.cell` (`declare::prompt_cell`): the cell, the hint "Describe the image…", the roles of `rounds::ROLES`, each with no `field`, as the model's workflow skill picks it, PNG, JPEG, and WebP references, and the settings Size (`size`, words), Images (`images`), and Seed (`seed`). The host draws the card and runs it; its runner, plan card, and picker are other sessions'. `edit_cell` changes a draft, one step of the strip's history; an edit to a step is the runner's and is ignored here. `insert_cell` adds a cell the tab does not have yet, one step. `cell_became_step`, when the agent has recorded a draft as a chain step, leaves that cell its pointer alone, here and in every step of the history that holds it, so no undo brings the draft back, and the file is written again without it. `select_cell` shows another of the tab's cells. Each time `cells` is sent, a draft with no step, no run, and no output says, as its `output`, the file its tab's image shown was opened from, its bottom-most linked layer's link path, so the host can find that take's round; it is never kept, and an image with no linked layer gives none. With no project open, where the host runs a cell itself, each image a `cell_result` made joins the gallery, linked to its file; no run is kept. In a project the runner runs every cell, and its rounds come with `takes`.

## Rounds and takes

`takes` sends every round one of the tab's cells made, oldest first. The strip shows each cell's rounds in the cells' order, each round a run labelled "Round N · <model>" (`FilmGroup`), then the gallery's own images as a run with no label; `film`'s `shown`, `select`, and `reorder` count the rounds' tiles first. A round has a tile for each image take (`image/png`, `image/jpeg`, or `image/webp`), sealed with `badge-check` when `canon`; one being made or waiting at the endpoint has a striped place for each take still to come, `pending` running since `started`; one that failed with no take has a failed place. Each take's file is read when it is new or changed on disk, for its size, `protocol::fingerprint` of its bytes, and its thumbnail, `<tab>-take-<n>.rgba`, 44 tall as an image's. Nothing of a round or a take is kept in the `.ipx`: a take whose report is gone leaves the strip at the next `takes`. A take's tile cannot be dragged among the gallery's.

A click on a take shows it in the working area, read from its file as a layer linked to it, with its pixels locked: painting is for Edit. Its notes are kept in the tab's file as editing state, by the take's file alone, its path and sha256 (`take_notes`), and come back when that take is read again from the same bytes, shown or sent in a next round; its pre-vis check is the take's while the program runs. A take with no notes and no change lets its frame go, and its files, when another is shown. The take shown is `chosen`. Its menu holds Next round from this (`take_next_round`), Ask the assistant to use this take (`take_ask`), Edit (`take_edit`), and Open report (`take_report`), which sends `open_report` with the take's round's request; each carries `take`, its path.

- **Next round from this** makes a draft of the take's round: its prompt, its references in order with their roles, and the take with the role Reference. When the take has notes, their words end the prompt as "Notes on the last round: 1. … 2. …", and the marked image (`Doc::marked_image`) is sent too, as a file, since nothing a model is sent lives only in `work/`: a PNG written in the project's `work/`, named after the image and " notes", free among the files there, the tab's file written first, and filed with `file_resource`, `from` the tab's file and then the take, with an `id` (`src/session/filing.rs`). The draft is made at once without it; when `filed` names the filed file, it joins the draft as a path, `image/png`, with the role "Notes on the last round", as part of the next round's own step: every copy of the draft in the history that is still the draft the next round made gets it too, so undo and redo keep it (`Gallery::amend_draft`). A filing that fails shows why as the failed notice, at the working area's bottom left. A current draft not yet run takes the next round in place; otherwise it is a new cell, shown. One step of the strip's history; the Prompt page is shown.
- **Ask the assistant to use this take** sends `ask_assistant` with "Put take N of round M, <path>, into the chain." and the take's file as its item, for the person to send.
- **Edit** adds the take to the gallery as a new image of one layer linked to its file, and shows it.

The toolbar's grid, "Round", and two rectangles, "references sheet", appear while the tab has rounds and work while a take is shown. The grid draws the take's round whole: its label, "Round N · <model> · N takes", its references in a column at the left with their roles, and its takes as a grid of numbered takes (`layers::layout::grid`), the one shown outlined in blue with a star. A Ctrl- or Shift-click on a take of another round draws both rounds one above the other, the older above, each labelled with its model and its prompt's first words, its takes in a row. The two rectangles draw the references sheet: "Round N · the references sent", each reference with its order and role, "1st, control: the room's layout.", those `left_out` under "Not sent", and the take beside them, "Take N". A round view is one image the working area's size, `<tab>-round.rgba`, with the camera put on it whole and the Hand tool: gestures on it do nothing, and the host's camera is not the take's. Its words are drawn with the app's own fonts.

## Items and renders

A project's files are filed, and nothing is declared as an item: no `items` is sent, and `render` answers any item "That is not an item of this image."

## The project

Every project is organized one way, the way Last order is, so `project` is read for its folder alone: a tab never written goes to the project's `work/` folder, and the `.ipx` never has its `source` inside a project, as Spumcake-Skills keeps what made a file. With no project open, a tab is saved where the person chooses and keeps its `source`.

A layer of pixels' menu has "Write out as PNG…" (`layer_write_png`): the layer at the image's size, clear where it does not reach, a plain PNG in the project's `work/`, the tab's file written first when it has none, named as the layer, free among the files there, or, with no file and no project, where the person chooses. Inside a project, `file_resource` then asks the runner to file it, with `request.py resource` the PNG `--from` the tab's file in `work/`, then the layer's own linked file when it is linked, then the file of every linked layer beneath it, lowest first, each once, edited or not, as paths in the project (`linked_from`), with an id, so a refusal shows as "The layer could not be filed".

## Publishing

A take enters the chain only through the assistant: the workspace publishes an edit, never a raw take, and never an edit while a take beneath it is not canon, as milestone 3 of the build plan has it.

- **Which artifact.** The host says which artifact, "<element>: <role>", a cell's step publishes: with `cell_became_step`'s `artifact`, or a round's `artifact`. Each is kept with the cell while the program runs, never in the file, as it is the chain's. An image of the gallery publishes as the artifact of the cell whose take one of its linked layers was made from (`Session::publishing_as`); one with no known artifact, and a take shown on its own, show no seal and no Publish.
- **The toolbar.** Beside the pre-vis check, as ig-publish shows them: the canon seal, `badge-check`, its tip "Published versions", shown on once the image has been published while the program runs, and Publish, dark, enabled only for an edit (`edited`): an image with a linked layer and a linked layer painted on, a mask, or any layer of its own besides.
- **Refused.** While any linked file beneath the image is a take whose `canon` is false, or a file outside the project, which cannot be canon, Publish sends nothing, however little was painted: a notice, at the working area's bottom left, says "Its inputs aren't canon.", names them, and, when one is a take, offers Ask the assistant, which sends `ask_assistant` with "Put take N of round M, <path>, into the chain." for the first and every such take as items. A linked file in the project that is no take of the tab's rounds goes to the runner, which finds it canon or refuses.
- **Publish.** The tab's file is written first; the image as it is now is written as a PNG in the project's `work/`, named by the tab's file and the image, "Shot 12 keyframes r1-1.png", made safe as a file's name, the same file each time it is published, overwritten as `work/` files are on save, even one the person saved under that name, which is `Publish.source`; and `publish` asks the host's runner to file it as the artifact, `from` the tab's file then every linked file in the image, lowest first, each once, as `file_resource` names them, and to publish it on the person's yes. `published` with a file shows "Published <file>, version N.", or "Published <file>." when it names no version, and marks the seal. With an empty file, after the card's Cancel or with the host's refusal card, which shows its own reason and Ask the assistant, the workspace stops waiting and says nothing. With no project open there is nothing to publish into, and Publish says "No project is open."
- **History.** The seal's click sends `canon_history`; the host answers with `history` and draws the history card under the seal itself, so the workspace keeps nothing of it. The card's Open sends `history_open`, which adds that file to the gallery as a linked image as Edit does, and its Revert to this `history_revert`, which sends `revert`. Versions and history are never kept in the file.

## Previews

`edits` declares `image/png`, `image/jpeg`, and `image/webp`. `preview` of such a file, in the project or whole, answers `previewed` with a raw thumbnail, `preview-<fingerprint of the path>.rgba` beside the images for the host, the file's shape fitted into 160 × 96 and no larger than itself, and no badge; a file that cannot be read answers with why.

## The `.ipx` file

A tab's gallery in a project is one `.ipx` file, written by itself about a second after a change, as Recipe Creation's recipes are. A writer thread in `session.rs` encodes and writes it, one file at a time and in order, from a snapshot the loop takes: an image whose pixels are unchanged since its layers were encoded, by its `generation`, such as after a change to an adjustment or a layer's settings alone, is not copied or looked at again. A due write starts only when the program is idle, never on a message, and never while a stroke is drawn. Save, Save As, quit, and closing a tab wait for the writer's earlier writes first, and an image changed while it was written stays unwritten. If the writer stops, its writes count as failed and are tried again. Which image is shown is written too, inside a project.

Names follow the conventions guide, through `protocol::untitled` and `protocol::free_name`. A new tab is "Untitled Image", or the first free name after it among the open tabs and the `.ipx` files in the project's folder, and a tab never written goes to a file named the same way. A new image on the filmstrip is free among the gallery's images, and a copy is "<name> copy", numbered the same way when taken.

1. The five bytes `IPX1` and a newline.
2. One line of compact JSON, the header, ended by a newline:
   - `version`: 4;
   - `shown`: the position of the image shown, from 0;
   - `cells`, from 0.15, the tab's cells, each a step pointer or a draft as `stored_cell` keeps it, and `cell`, the one the Prompt page shows;
   - `take_notes`, from 0.15, the notes marked on takes: each `path`, relative to the project, `sha256`, the take's bytes they were marked on, and `notes`, as an image's; never the take or its record;
   - `source`, with no project open, the cell and image the gallery was opened from as new work;
   - `pictures`, the stored spelling of the images: every image, in filmstrip order. Each has:
     - `name`;
     - `width` and `height`: the image's size in pixels;
     - `active`: the active layer's id, if any;
     - `notes`, when it has any or they are hidden: `pins`, each `number`, `x` and `y` in image pixels, and `text`; `marks`, each `points` (`[x, y]` in image pixels), `width`, and `colour` (RGBA); and `hidden` when the Notes row's eye hides them;
     - `previs`, when its check was ever set: `render`, its `path` and `sha256` as a link's, `mode` (`edges` or `see_through`), `opacity` (0 to 1), and `on`;
     - `layers`: the top-level layers and groups, bottom to top. Each has `id`, `name`, `visible`, `blend` (`normal`, `multiply`, `screen`, `overlay`, `darken`, or `lighten`), `opacity` and `fill` (0 to 1), `locks` (`transparency`, `pixels`, `position`, `all`), and `offset` (`[x, y]` in image pixels), and `kind`: `pixels`, `group`, `text`, `curves`, or `grade`. A pixel layer has `png`: `[start, length]` of its PNG among the bytes after the header line. A group has `children` instead, bottom to top, and no `png`. A text has `text`, its `words`, `style` (`face` `inter` or `mono`, `size`, `bold`, `italic`, `underline`, `color`, `align`, `line_height`, `letter_spacing`, `all_caps`), and `width` when it wraps, its box's corner being `offset`, and no pixels. A Curves layer has `curves` (`rgb`, `red`, `green`, `blue`, each points from 0 to 1), and a Color Grade layer `grade` (`exposure`, `contrast`, `saturation`, `temperature`). Any node may have `mask`: its `id`, `name`, `feather`, `visible`, and `png`, a grey PNG. A pixel layer made from a file has `link`, its `path` and `sha256`, and `edited` when painted on; an unedited one has no `png`. Layer and mask ids are unique within their image.
3. Each pixel layer's pixels as an RGBA PNG, and each mask's as a grey PNG, one after another, every image's in turn.

Version 3, 0.13's and 0.14's file, is the same without links, `notes`, or `previs`, and opens as before. Version 2, 0.11's and 0.12's file, is also without `kind`, texts, adjustments, or masks, and opens as before.

Version 1, 0.10's file, has one image's `width`, `height`, `active`, and `layers` in the header itself, with no list of images or `shown`. It opens as a gallery of one, named as the file is, and is written as version 4 after its next change.

A layer's pixels may be any size and sit anywhere: a new layer is the image's size at `[0, 0]`, an image opened as a layer keeps its own size, centred, and a moved layer keeps its pixels past the image's edge.
