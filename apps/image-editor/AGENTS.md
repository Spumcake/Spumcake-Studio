# Image Editor — sub-application

The Image Editor edits a gallery of layered pictures per tab: a filmstrip of pictures to add, open, duplicate, rename, delete, and reorder, and on the picture shown brush and eraser with pen pressure, the clone stamp and the healing brush, rectangle and lasso selections, move, moving selected pixels, crop, eyedropper, text layers, masks, Curves and Color Grade adjustment layers, layers and groups with blend modes, opacity, fill, and locks, undo, autosave in the project, and PNG and JPEG in and out. The host draws the shown picture and the filmstrip as a `raster` tab, owns the camera and the pointer, and sends each finished gesture, the keyboard while a text is typed in, and each click on the strip. The contracts are Inpainter Studio 0.10's, 0.11's, and 0.13's `systems.md` in the team repository.

## Rules for this sub-application

- It is its own program. Its crate depends on `protocol`, `serde`, `serde_json`, `image` (PNG and JPEG only), and `ab_glyph` (pinned at 0.2.32, for text layers, as the owner approved on 2026-10-31) and nothing else, never on the host or on another sub-application.
- Text is drawn only from the fonts in `assets/fonts/`, built into the program with `include_bytes!`: Inter, its roman and italic variable files, bold through the weight axis, and Hack, egui's monospace, whose bold and italic are drawn by thickening and slanting it. Never the computer's fonts: a future web version must find every font in the app.
- Everything the host shows for it is declared through the protocol: its tabs, toolbar and settings row, menu, keys, the Layers page, and the raster contents. Nothing about it is hard-coded in the host.
- It owns every picture of a gallery, and every pixel, layer, selection, crop box, step of history, and file. The host only draws the picture and thumbnails this program writes.
- Each picture keeps its own history, and Undo acts on the picture shown. The filmstrip's changes (add, duplicate, remove, rename, reorder) are steps of the strip's own history, which Undo acts on after the host sends the strip's `focus`, until choosing a picture on the strip, a gesture, a panel edit, or a command on the picture gives the keyboard back. A thumbnail click is `focus` then `select`, so Undo after it is the shown picture's.
- It runs with no host, driven only through its standard input and standard output.
- It speaks the protocol defined in `protocol/` and nothing else. When it needs something the protocol cannot say, change the protocol, the host, and the scaffolding skill together. Do not work around the protocol.
- `manifest.json` registers it with the host. Its id, folder, crate, and program names are the same.

## Layout

- `src/picture.rs` — the picture: the tree of layers and groups (settings only), each node's kind (pixels, text, Curves, or Color Grade) and mask, each layer's and mask's pixels by id, the blend modes, the locks, and the composite, with each feathered mask blurred once and each text drawn once until it changes. Curves use `draw::ink`'s monotone cubic, copied line for line, so the host's drawn curve is the one applied; a curve's tangents are worked out once, and each adjustment's tables are made once for a composite and kept by their settings. The composite is made in bands of rows, one per core, each a few rows at a time so they stay in the core's cache, and can start from the picture as the first top-level nodes combine it (`Beneath`), which gives the same bytes as combining every node.
- `src/text.rs` — a text's words, style, and box: its layout, following Simple Sketch's rules in `draw/src/text.rs`, drawn with ab_glyph; the caret, the selection's spans, and the character under a point.
- `src/paint.rs` — the brush and eraser's coverage, which skips pixels already fully covered, and how a stroke changes pixels; what the clone stamp copies and what the healing brush paints; the selection's coverage mask, its four ways of joining, feather, and outline.
- `src/doc.rs` — one picture as it is edited: every change a person or the assistant makes, each one undo step that keeps only the region that changed, and the composite brought up to date only where it changed. It holds the stroke being drawn, the selected pixels being moved, and the text being typed in. A value a layer already has changes nothing and is no edit. Its `generation` changes whenever pixels may have changed, and not for a change to the tree's settings alone, so what was made from the pixels holds while it stays: the picture beneath a held slider's layer, kept only while the slider is held, so each value combines that layer and what lies above it again; a Curves layer's histograms, of what lies beneath it, so dragging the curve never makes them again; and a file's copy of the layers.
- `src/gallery.rs` — one tab's gallery: its pictures in order, the one shown, and the filmstrip's history, which holds a removed picture, with its own history, until it is put back.
- `src/session.rs` — the tabs, the protocol messages, the filmstrip's commands and which of strip or picture has the keyboard, files and autosave, and the pictures and thumbnails written for the host.
- `src/declare.rs` — the toolbar and settings row as the approved views show them, the panel's Layers, Properties, and Adjustments pages, the filmstrip, the menu, the keys, and the tab list.
- `src/assist.rs` — the assistant's "Image Editor" skill: the gallery's tools, `look` at any picture, and the shown picture's layers, texts, masks, and adjustments. Painting, cloning, healing, and moving pixels stay the person's.
- `src/files.rs` — the `.ipx` file, PNG and JPEG in and out, and whole-file writes.

## What it writes for the host

After every change it sends `raster`, with the filmstrip in `film`. The picture shown is `<tab>-<picture>.rgba`, one file for each picture of the gallery, in a folder of the session's own in memory, `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/inpainter/image-editor/<process>-<number>/`, when the system has that folder, as the files are written many times a second, read by the host, and never kept; it stays there when a project is named, goes when the program ends, and a folder left by a program no longer running is removed when one starts. Without it, the file is in `<project>/.inpainter/cache/image-editor/`, or a scratch folder under the system's temporary folder with no project. The picture is width × height × 4 bytes, RGBA with straight alpha, rows top to bottom, replaced whole by writing `<name>.partial` and renaming it. Each layer's thumbnail is `<tab>-<picture>-<layer id>.rgba` beside it, the picture's shape fitted into 56 × 40, and each picture's filmstrip thumbnail is `<tab>-film-<picture>.rgba`, its composite at 44 pixels tall and no more than 176 wide; `<picture>` is a number unique within the tab for this run. Each is written again only when its picture changed, so a picture shown again is the file and revision the host already holds. While a slider is held, the thumbnails and the filmstrip's wait, and are written when it is let go.

A held slider's or curve's values, `property` with `preview`, are applied as they come, in order with everything else, and the picture and panel they make are sent once for all the lines waiting, in `main.rs`, as a stroke's parts are; a value that changes nothing sends nothing. Any other message first sends what the values changed, so its answers come after them, except a panel edit or `finish_edit`, which send everything themselves. The value let go, `property` without `preview` or `finish_edit`, is always applied and sent, and the held values and it are one step. A changed picture's `patch` names the part that differs from the revision the host was last told, so the host reads only those rows.

A brush or eraser stroke arrives as it is drawn, in `stroke_part` gestures, and `doc.rs` paints each part at once with the finished stroke's brush, without a step of history: each pixel keeps the most coverage any piece gave it, and is painted again from the layer as it was before the stroke. The lines waiting are all read before the picture is sent, in `main.rs`. While a stroke is drawn, a view writes only the rows it changed, to `<tab>-<picture>-<revision>.patch`, named in its `patch.file`, and the last 32 are kept for a host that falls behind. Views say the picture is still being made until the program is idle, when `tick` writes the picture file whole under the same revision, sends the view without a file, and removes the row files. The finished `stroke` makes the painted stroke one step without painting it again; `stroke_cancel`, or any message that could change the picture, takes it off. The host's ping, the camera, and the viewport leave it be. Revisions start from the time the program started and rise with every file written, so a picture from an earlier run, or another picture of the gallery, is never taken for the current one.

The clone stamp and the healing brush are declared as `sampler`. Alt-click sends `source`; a stroke's offset is from its first point to the source, kept for the next stroke when Aligned is on (healing always is), and declared as `offset` while it holds. Both copy from the layers up to and including the active one as the stroke began, so adjustments above are left out and a stroke never copies itself, and both paint part by part as the brush does. The stamp's release becomes the step as it was painted; healing, `src(p+o) − blur(src)(p+o) + light(p)` with `light` the picture's normalised blur leaving out the stroke's own pixels, heals the whole stroke again on release, and that is its step.

With a selection, the Move tool's `drag_part` lifts the selected pixels off the active layer, leaving it clear there, and draws them and the selection moved; `drag` sets them down as one step, and `stroke_cancel` puts them back.

A mask belongs to its node, as `Node.mask`: grey pixels by the mask's own id, at the node's offset, which multiply the node's coverage, white showing and black hiding, outside its pixels showing. It moves, reorders, crops, and is deleted with its node, and has its own row directly above it in the Layers list. Chosen from that row, the brush paints the lightness of its colour into it and the eraser paints black; the stamp and healing do not act on it. An adjustment changes what is already combined beneath it within its group, mixed in by its opacity and its mask; groups keep combining their layers by themselves, so an adjustment inside a group changes only that group.

The T tool's click sends `click` and a drag `rect`: a new text, or the text whose box was clicked, is typed in, and `RasterView.text` carries its box, caret, and selected spans. The keyboard and the pointer inside the box arrive as `raster_text`; Copy and Cut are answered with `clipboard`. Each editing session is one step: a style change, a box drag (`text_box`), or Undo first makes the typing its own step, and Escape, a click outside, another tool, or any other change ends editing. A text left empty is taken away.

The filmstrip's actions are `film_focus`, `film_select` (`value`: a position), `film_reorder` (`from`, `to`), `film_new`, `film_open`, and each thumbnail's `film_duplicate`, `film_rename`, and `film_delete` (`picture`: a position). Rename is a row with `rename` set: the host renames in place and sends the name as `value`. A `film_rename` with no `value` tells the person to ask the assistant, since the protocol's questions have no text field.

## The `.ipx` file

A tab's gallery in a project is one `.ipx` file, written by itself about a second after a change, as Mission Control's notebooks are. A writer thread in `session.rs` encodes and writes it, one file at a time and in order, from a snapshot the loop takes: a picture whose pixels are unchanged since its layers were encoded, by its `generation`, such as after a change to an adjustment or a layer's settings alone, is not copied or looked at again. A due write starts only when the program is idle, never on a message, and never while a stroke is drawn. Save, Save As, quit, and closing a tab wait for the writer's earlier writes first, and a picture changed while it was written stays unwritten. If the writer stops, its writes count as failed and are tried again. Which picture is shown is written too, inside a project.

1. The five bytes `IPX1` and a newline.
2. One line of compact JSON, the header, ended by a newline:
   - `version`: 3;
   - `shown`: the position of the picture shown, from 0;
   - `pictures`: every picture, in filmstrip order. Each has:
     - `name`;
     - `width` and `height`: the picture's size in pixels;
     - `active`: the active layer's id, if any;
     - `layers`: the top-level layers and groups, bottom to top. Each has `id`, `name`, `visible`, `blend` (`normal`, `multiply`, `screen`, `overlay`, `darken`, or `lighten`), `opacity` and `fill` (0 to 1), `locks` (`transparency`, `pixels`, `position`, `all`), and `offset` (`[x, y]` in picture pixels), and `kind`: `pixels`, `group`, `text`, `curves`, or `grade`. A pixel layer has `png`: `[start, length]` of its PNG among the bytes after the header line. A group has `children` instead, bottom to top, and no `png`. A text has `text`, its `words`, `style` (`face` `inter` or `mono`, `size`, `bold`, `italic`, `underline`, `color`, `align`, `line_height`, `letter_spacing`, `all_caps`), and `width` when it wraps, its box's corner being `offset`, and no pixels. A Curves layer has `curves` (`rgb`, `red`, `green`, `blue`, each points from 0 to 1), and a Color Grade layer `grade` (`exposure`, `contrast`, `saturation`, `temperature`). Any node may have `mask`: its `id`, `name`, `feather`, `visible`, and `png`, a grey PNG. Layer and mask ids are unique within their picture.
3. Each pixel layer's pixels as an RGBA PNG, and each mask's as a grey PNG, one after another, every picture's in turn.

Version 2, 0.11's and 0.12's file, is the same without `kind`, texts, adjustments, or masks, and opens as before.

Version 1, 0.10's file, has one picture's `width`, `height`, `active`, and `layers` in the header itself, with no `pictures` or `shown`. It opens as a gallery of one, named as the file is, and is written as version 3 after its next change.

A layer's pixels may be any size and sit anywhere: a new layer is the picture's size at `[0, 0]`, an image opened as a layer keeps its own size, centred, and a moved layer keeps its pixels past the picture's edge.

## Checks

`cargo test -p image-editor` runs the unit tests and `tests/session.rs`, which starts the built program and drives it through protocol lines alone. `cargo test --release -p image-editor -- --ignored --nocapture` times a stroke and its composite on a 1920 × 1080 image, and a held slider's value, from its line to the picture and panel sent, at 1370 × 1148 with four layers (`a_held_slider_keeps_up`: under 16 ms for a Color Grade and 25 ms for Curves).
