# Video Generator — sub-application

The Video Generator is the Image Generator for video, as the owner set on 2026-11-16: a gallery of takes per tab, a filmstrip of takes to open, duplicate, rename, delete, and reorder, each a link to a video file that is never copied or written; the shown take at a frame the keys step; two takes side by side at the same time, which at 100% is the before/after crop; every take as a row of eight frames, the frame strips; undo for the gallery and the view; autosave in the project as a `.vgx` file; and, as the Image Generator's, the Prompt page's cell, the rounds of takes its requests made on the filmstrip, and a take's menu; playing the take shown with its sound, under the transport row, with its waveform; notes at a time, pins and marks on a take's frame, each a cue on the scrubber; the shot's animatic: a Start frame taken from it at the playhead, and a round's take shown over it, the pre-vis check; finishing a take, Retime… and Upscale…, and comparing it with its source; sound layers mixed with the take's own sound; and Deliver…. The host draws the working area's image and the filmstrip as a `raster` tab and sends each click on the strip, the keys, the Move tool's drag, and the notes tool's clicks, words, and marks. The contracts are milestones 1 and 4 of the Image and Video build plan in the team repository, the lifecycle's `request-page.md`, and the Studio-as-a-setup contract; the wipe over the animatic comes with a later stage.

## Rules for this sub-application

- It is its own program. Its crate depends on `protocol`, `serde`, `serde_json`, `media` (decoding video, shared with the Storyboard Generator), `image`, and `layers` (linked files, laying out takes, and drawing the views' words in Inter, which ships there, shared with the Image Generator), as Studio 0.15's plan approved, and nothing else, never on the host or on another sub-application.
- Everything the host shows for it is declared through the protocol: its tabs, toolbar, menu, keys, the panel's Prompt page, the filmstrip with its rounds, and the raster contents. Nothing about it is hard-coded in the host.
- A take changes nothing until it is put in use. The workspace keeps editing state only: its `.vgx` holds a cell's step pointer or a draft, never a round, a take a request made, a prompt sent, a run, or a record of what made anything; a draft keeps its own prompt only until `cell_became_step` leaves the cell its step pointer alone, as the Image Generator's As built allows. Rounds and takes are read from what the host sends with `takes`, every time.
- It owns every tab's gallery, its history, its file, and every frame it decodes. The host only draws the image and thumbnails this program writes.
- A take is a link to its file, `layers::link::Link`: the path as the `.vgx` names it and the file's sha256. The file is read, never written, moved, or copied.
- The gallery has one history. Adding, duplicating, removing, renaming, and moving takes, changing the view (one take, side by side, or frame strips), a draft edited, a next round, a finishing draft, a take's notes (a new pin with its words, a mark, a pin moved, a pin taken away), and the take's own sound and the sound layers (one added, muted, renamed, deleted, moved, its gain or start set) are its steps. Choosing a take on its own, the frame, the zoom, the tool, and playing are not.
- It runs with no host, driven only through its standard input and standard output.
- It speaks the protocol defined in `protocol/` and nothing else. When it needs something the protocol cannot say, change the protocol, the host, and the scaffolding skill together. Do not work around the protocol.
- `manifest.json` registers it with the host. Its id, folder, crate, and program names are the same.

## Layout

- `src/gallery.rs` — one tab's gallery: its takes in order, each with its link, its file, and what `media::Info` says it is; the take shown; the view; and the history, which holds a removed take until it is put back. Side by side names its other take by a number unique in the tab, so it holds through reorders, and shows one take while that take is gone.
- `src/view.rs` — what the working area shows, as a `Scene`: where each frame is drawn and at what size it is decoded, laid out with `layers::layout`, and side by side the second take's frame at the first's time (`seconds_at`, `frame_at`); and drawing it into one image, with `layers::layout::place` where a frame is drawn at its decoded size, and scaled smoothly within its half where it is zoomed, with the animatic's lines and edges laid over the take's frame when the pre-vis check is on (`Animatic`), and the notes over it.
- `src/notes.rs` — notes drawn over a take's frame in the view (`NotesOver`), the open pin's card and its words being typed, and the marked frame a next round sends; the discs, numbers, and marks by `layers::notes`, the words by `layers::label`.
- `src/decode.rs` — frames decoded off the message loop: the frame shown, one or two, from a `media::Seeker` for each file, the newest request winning; the frame strips and the filmstrip's thumbnails from one thread of its own, thumbnails first, then the strips last asked for, in order.
- `src/rounds.rs` — a tab's rounds as `takes` sends them: the strip's tiles, each take read through `media` when new or changed on disk, with its sha256; and the take shown.
- `src/session.rs` — the tabs, the protocol messages, the filmstrip's commands, the frame, the zoom and its drag, files and autosave, and the image and thumbnails written for the host.
- `src/session/requests.rs` — the cells' messages, `takes`, showing a take, a take's menu, and `render`'s answer.
- `src/session/filing.rs` — the files a tab writes for a draft, the marked frame and a Start frame taken from the animatic, written beside its file in `work/`, filed with `file_resource`, and put in the draft when `filed` names them.
- `src/session/play.rs` — playing the take shown with its sound: the Player, play and pause, a step, a scrub, the host's `shown`, and the waveform written for the transport row.
- `src/session/notes.rs` — the toolbar's tools and the notes tool: a pin put, opened, moved, and typed in, a mark drawn part by part, each a step of the gallery's history; the notes shown at the time shown; and each pin's cue.
- `src/finishing.rs` — every finishing endpoint, its settings, a finishing round's label, and the finishing words pending the owner, in one place.
- `src/session/mix.rs` — the take's own sound and the tab's sound layers: the Layers and Properties pages, Open sound as layer…, the tracks the Player mixes, the rows under the scrubber, and the mix's arguments (`mix_args`).
- `src/session/deliver.rs` — Deliver…: sound layers filed, the mix made, and the host's card answered, with the notices.
- `src/session/animatic.rs` — the shot's animatic: the Start frame row's menu, the frame at the playhead filed and put in the Start frame, and the pre-vis check's animatic frame for the take shown.
- `src/waveform.rs` — a take's sound decoded once for each file on a thread of its own, its peaks, its waveform drawn at any size, and a WAV's cue points read from its RIFF chunks (`wav_cues`).
- `src/declare.rs` — the toolbar with its tools and the notes tool's settings row, the menu, the keys, the filmstrip with its rounds and a take's menu, the transport row (`transport`), the panel's Prompt page (`prompt_cell`), and the tab list.
- `src/assist.rs` — the assistant's "Video Generator" tool group.
- `src/files.rs` — the `.vgx` file and whole-file writes.
- `src/preview.rs` — the previews the host asks for: a video's first frame, small, and its length; and a sound's waveform, its length, and a WAV's cues.

## What it writes for the host

The loop in `main.rs` wakes after 4 ms without a line while frames or a sound are being decoded, or a take plays, to take them in as they come and follow the Player's clock, and after 250 ms otherwise, for `tick`, which writes a project's changes that are due. The host's `shown`, which revision it drew, is read as any line: while playing, it keeps the Player's lead and paces the frames.

The tab's image is the working area's own size, as `viewport` and each command report it, made even. The view is the workspace's own: every `raster` says `camera_owner` is the workspace, so the host never moves its camera on its own, and its first view, and the first after the area changes, carry the camera, origin `[0, 0]` and zoom 1, so the image is drawn at its own pixels rather than fitted with the host's margin. The host sends the wheel, a pan, and the magnifier's click as `view` gestures instead. A `view` gesture zooms the shown take by `zoom_by` about `at`, so the take's point under it stays there, starting from the zoom it is fitted at, and moves it by `pan_by`, image pixels right and down (`Session::view_by`); frame strips, and a pan of a fitted take, change nothing. The zoom menu (Fit, 50%, 100%, 200%), the `view` gestures, and the Move tool's drag, `drag_part` from the press, choose what the image shows, so a 4K take is never decoded whole to be shown small: each frame is decoded through `media` at the size it is drawn, never larger than its file. Fitted, a take is as large as the area less 40 pixels around, and never larger than its own pixels. Zoomed, it is drawn at that zoom about a point kept in the middle, as far as its edges let it be, and the drag moves that point. Side by side, the area is halved with 16 between, and at a zoom both takes are drawn at the wider one's scale about the same point, so they show the same region of each: at 100%, the larger take at its own pixels, which is the before/after crop. Frame strips put every take's row, in filmstrip order, eight columns with 8 between across the area, packed from the top as vg-frame-strips shows, each row under the last with 18 pixels for its times and 8 between (`view::strip_cells`), sharing the area's height only when the rows would not fit; each frame is at the end of an eighth of the take's length, so the last is its last frame, and its time, "1.0 s", is written under it. Side by side, each take's name is written small in gray above it, as vg-takes-compare shows. The words are drawn with `layers::label`, never the computer's fonts.

After every change it sends `raster`, with the filmstrip in `film`, the pointer of the tool chosen, and a pin's words while they are typed (`text`). The image is `<tab>-view.rgba`, written by `media::write_frame`, in a folder of the session's own in memory, `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/inpainter/video-generator/<process>-<number>/`, when the system has that folder, as it is written for every frame stepped, read by the host, and never kept; it stays there when a project is named, goes when the program ends, and a folder left by a program no longer running is removed when one starts. Without it, the image is in `<project>/.spumcake/cache/video-generator/`, or a scratch folder under the system's temporary folder with no project. It is width × height × 4 bytes, RGBA, rows top to bottom, on white, replaced whole by writing `<name>.partial` and renaming it. A frame not yet decoded keeps the image last written, with its revision, until every frame of the new view has come, so stepping never shows half an image; frame strips are drawn as their frames come. A take whose file is missing or unreadable is drawn as a grey box. Each file's filmstrip thumbnail is its first frame at 44 pixels tall and no more than 176 wide, as the Image Generator's, `film-<number>.rgba` beside the image, made once for each file, so a duplicate shares it. Revisions start from the time the program started and rise with every file written.

The keys step the frame: Left and Right one frame, Shift with them one second of the shown take, Home and End its first and last; `seek` shows a frame. Each pauses first, as the transport row's step and scrub do. Each tab keeps one frame, the shown take's, which names a time: side by side, the second take shows its frame at that time in seconds, at its own frame rate, and a shorter take holds its last frame. The toolbar's grid button turns the frame strips on and off, or, while a round's take is shown, its round whole. Its tools are Move (V), whose drag pans a zoomed view, Hand (H), Zoom (Z), and Notes, after the magnifier with no key, as the Image Generator's; `tool` chooses one by its `tool`, `move`, `hand`, `zoom`, or `notes`.

The filmstrip's actions are `film_focus`, `film_select` (`value`: a position, and `extend` set for a Ctrl- or Shift-click, which shows that take beside the one shown; a plain click shows a take alone), `film_reorder` (`from`, `to`), the + tile's `film_open`, and each thumbnail's `film_duplicate`, `film_rename`, and `film_delete` (`take`: a position). Rename is a row with `rename` set: the host renames in place and sends the name as `value`. A `film_rename` with no `value` tells the person to ask the assistant, since the protocol's questions have no text field.

The assistant's "Video Generator" tool group, `takes`, has `list_takes` (each take's position, name, file, size, frames, frames per second, and length, which is shown, the view, and the frame), `open_video` (a video file by its path, absolute or in the project, added at the end and shown), `show_take` (a take by its position, at a frame), and `set_view` (`single`, `strips`, or `side_by_side` `with` another take). Opening a take and changing the view are steps of the gallery's history, each call its own.

## Playing

The take shown plays with its sound, as the Storyboard Generator plays its film (`src/session/play.rs`). The sound plays in `media`'s Player, opened once for the session at 48 000 samples a second and the take's frame rate, `Player::silent` when `INPAINTER_NO_SOUND` is set, as tests set it; and the frame shown is always the one the Player's clock names (`frame_to_show`), never one a timer of this program's counts. Each new frame is asked of the take's seeker at the size it is drawn, as a stepped frame is, and the image is written when it comes. The host's `shown` gives the delay between a frame sent and drawn, whose mean over the last 30 is the Player's lead, and paces the frames: while two are sent and not yet drawn, the next waits, up to a quarter of a second. Playing stops at the take's last frame; play from the last frame starts from the first.

The transport row is declared on every `raster` while a take, or two side by side, is shown (`declare::transport`), never for frame strips: the take's `length` in seconds and `fps`; `time`, the Player's clock while playing, or else the frame shown's; `playing`; `clock` the workspace's, so the host draws the time given and never moves it; `play`, `transport_play`, which plays or pauses as its `value` says; and `step`, `transport_step`, a frame back or forward by its `value`. A scrub comes as `transport_time`, a time in seconds, shown as its frame. A step and a scrub, like the keys and `seek`, pause first and play that frame's slice of sound, never for a take with no sound, so a silent take opens no output; a take with no sound plays silently. Playing, pausing, a step, and a scrub never change the gallery or its history, and any change to the gallery, another tab, or another take of a round pauses first. Side by side, both takes show their frames at the one clock's time, the sound the take shown first's, and a shorter take holds its last frame; the transport's `length` and `fps` are the take shown first's, and its step is one of that take's frames. Each pin of the take shown is a cue on the scrubber (`Session::note_cues`): `at` its time, `label` its number and time to a tenth, "1 — 3.4 s" (`layers::notes::Pin::moment`), since the host draws a cue's label alone, and `colour` the notes' red. An open pin's card starts its words with the same, "1 — 3.4 s · ".

A take's sound is decoded whole, once for each file, on a thread of its own (`src/waveform.rs`), with the loudest sample of each of 4 096 spans, and kept by the file's sha256. Its waveform is the transport row's one row, "Sound", gray bars on clear, 24 pixels tall and the working area's width, as the scrubber runs under the image, drawn from the peaks once for each sound and width as `wave-<n>.rgba` beside the images for the host. A take with no sound has no row.

## Notes

Notes at a time, as vg-notes-at-time shows them: the Image Generator's notes on a take's frame, each also at a moment, since in a video a note belongs to a moment as well as a place. The notes tool, `chat-bubble`, has a settings row as the Image Generator's: Colour (`notes_colour`, `value` `[r, g, b]`), the marks' colour, and Pin or Draw (`notes_mode`, `value` 0 or 1). With Pin, the pointer is the text tool's: a click on the frame pauses, then puts a pin numbered after the highest at that point and at the time shown, `at` in seconds, or opens the pin shown there; its words are typed through `raster_text` while `RasterView.text` gives their box and caret, typed words going after them, Backspace taking off the last letter, Backspace or Delete in a pin with no words taking it away, and Enter, Escape, or another click ending the typing. A drag from a pin moves it. With Draw, the pointer is a brush of the marks' colour, 4 pixels on the screen: `stroke_part` draws a mark at the time shown as it comes, and `stroke` finishes it. A new pin with its words, a mark, a pin moved, and a pin taken away are each one step of the gallery's history, and undoing one leaves the take shown, a round's take among them.

A note shows over the frame only while the time shown is within half a second of its `at` (`layers::notes::NEAR`); a pin being typed in always shows. The pin's disc and number and the marks are drawn by `layers::notes`, at the take's size scaled as its frame is drawn, and the open pin's card, its words wrapped at 320 pixels, by `layers::label` (`src/notes.rs`). Frame strips show no notes, and side by side shows the first take's.

Notes belong to a take's file, by the take's path and sha256, for the gallery's takes and a round's alike, and are kept in the `.vgx` as `take_notes`. They are editing state only, and come back whenever the same bytes are shown again.

Next round from this carries a take's notes as the Image Generator's does: the pins' words end the prompt, "Notes on the last round: 1. less rain (1.0 s)", each with its time (`Notes::as_timed_words`); and the take's frame at the first note's time, at its own size, with the notes of that moment on it and no card, is sent too, as a file, since nothing a model is sent lives only in `work/`. It is written as a PNG in the project's `work/`, "<tab> notes.png", free among the files there, the tab's file written first, and filed with `file_resource`, `from` the tab's file and then the take, with an `id` (`src/session/filing.rs`). The draft is made at once without it; when `filed` names the filed file, it joins the draft as a path, `image/png`, with the role "Notes on the last round", as part of the next round's own step: every copy of the draft in the history that is still the draft the next round made gets it too, so undo and redo keep it (`Gallery::amend_draft`). A filing that fails shows why as the failed notice, at the working area's bottom left.

## The animatic

A shot's animatic is the Prompt page's Motion reference, and a round's take is shown over its round's, as vg-frame-from and vg-take-over-animatic show (`src/session/animatic.rs`).

**The Start frame from the animatic.** The Prompt page's cell declares `reference_menu`, the rows each reference's menu adds between the host's Choose… and Clear, sent with the reference's index as `value`, which this ignores: "The animatic's frame at the playhead" (`frame_from_animatic`). It takes the draft's first reference whose role is Motion reference, as a file; the role is its menu word alone or before the person's words, "Motion reference, the walk". It takes that video's frame at the Video Generator's own time, the Player's while playing or else the frame shown's, decoded through `media` at the video's own size, and writes it as a PNG in the project's `work/`, "<tab> start frame.png", free among the files there, the tab's file written first, then files it with `file_resource`, `from` the tab's file and then the animatic. When `filed` names the filed file, it goes in the draft's Start frame as a path, `image/png`, with the role Start frame, in place of any Start frame: one step of the gallery's history. A filing that fails shows why as the failed notice. While the cell is a step, or its draft has no Motion reference, the row is declared off. Its tip, `MenuEntry.tip`, says why: `session::NO_ANIMATIC`, "Add the shot's animatic as a Motion reference first.", pending the owner's word. The host's Prompt page shows the frame as not canon until it is filed and published. Open in the Image Generator (`frame_open_image`), the menu's second row in vg-frame-from, sends `open_in` with the reference, by its place among the cell's files and items as the host counts them, when it is an image, a Start frame taken from the animatic among them: its path; the host opens it in the workspace whose `edits` declares the type. Any other reference opens nothing.

**A take over its animatic.** The toolbar's pre-vis check, the cube with the tip "Pre-vis check" as the Image Generator's (`previs_toggle`), shows a round's take over its round's Motion reference at the same time: the animatic's pre-vis edges in cyan, as the Image Generator's pre-vis check draws them, then its line art, every pixel darker than 128 in grey, in white (`layers::edges::over_animatic`), as Spumcake-Skills' `overlay.py` lays them with one animatic as both. With no Motion reference, it shows the take over the round's Start frame, its edges only, in cyan, as the Image Generator's check draws them (`layers::edges::check`), since a colour keyframe's darks are no line art. A round's roles may be free words, as Last order's requests name them, "Video 1, the motion and the acting" or "the start frame" (`animatic::round_animatic`): its Motion reference is the reference whose role is Motion reference, or else its first video whose role is none of the Prompt page's other menu words, so a take sent back as Reference is none; its Start frame is the reference whose role is Start frame or begins "the start frame". The frame is captioned under it "Over the animatic · <the tab's name>" (`animatic::OVER_ANIMATIC`, pending the owner's word), the tab's name because it is always at hand, where a round's step has a name only once it is in the chain. The animatic's frame is that of the take's time, its last when the take runs past it, decoded off the loop by its own seeker at the size the take's frame is decoded, as a take's frame is, and the image is drawn once both have come; while playing, each frame drawn gets the animatic's frame at its time. A take not in a round, a round with neither reference, its round shown whole, and a gallery's take shown first beside it have the button off. It is on or off for the tab while the program runs, never kept.

## The Prompt page

Each tab has cells, kept in its `.vgx` as `cells`, and the one the Prompt page shows, `cell`, as the Image Generator's are: each a pointer to a chain step, `step` with nothing else, or a draft before its first run, its own prompt, references, settings, model, and endpoint, as `layers::cells::stored_cell` keeps it. A tab with none makes an empty video draft, which is no step, its Size the gallery's first take's, or its rounds' takes', or "1280 × 720", and its Length 8 seconds. They are sent with `cells`, before the panel that names one, whenever they change. The panel's fourth page, Prompt, declares `Properties.cell` (`declare::prompt_cell`), as vg-request-inputs shows it: the hint `HINT`, "Describe the shot…", the roles Start frame and End frame, each a row of its own (`slot`), then Reference, Motion reference, and Sound, each with no `field`, as the model's workflow skill picks it; PNG, JPEG, WebP, MP4, MOV, WAV, MP3, and FLAC references; and the settings Size (`size`, words), Length (`length`, a plain field of 1 to 20 seconds), and Seed (`seed`, a plain field). Layers and Properties hold the take's own sound and the sound layers (Sound layers, below); Adjustments is declared off. The host draws the card and runs it. `edit_cell` changes a draft, one step of the gallery's history; an edit to a step is the runner's and is ignored here. `insert_cell` adds a cell the tab does not have yet, one step. `select_cell` shows another of the tab's cells. Each time `cells` is sent, a draft with no step, no run, and no output says, as its `output`, the file of the gallery's take shown, its link path, so the host can find that take's round; it is never kept, and a round's take shown, which has its round already, or a tab with no take gives none. `cell_became_step`, when the agent has recorded a draft as a chain step, leaves that cell its pointer alone, here and in every step of the history that holds it, so no undo brings the draft back; the artifact it publishes is kept while the program runs, never in the file. With no project open, where the host runs a cell itself, each video a `cell_result` made joins the gallery as a take linking its file; no run is kept. In a project the runner runs every cell, and its rounds come with `takes`.

## Rounds and takes

`takes` sends every round one of the tab's cells made, oldest first, as for the Image Generator. The strip shows each cell's rounds in the cells' order, each round a run labelled "Round N · <model>" (`FilmGroup`), then the gallery's own takes as a run with no label; `film`'s `shown`, `select`, and `reorder` count the rounds' tiles first, and a round's tile cannot be dragged among the gallery's. A round has a tile for each video take (`video/mp4`, `video/quicktime`, or `video/webm`), its first frame with its length, "2.0 s", sealed with `badge-check` when `canon`; one being made or waiting at the endpoint has a striped place for each take still to come, `pending` running since `started`; one that failed with no take has a failed place. Each take's file is read through `media` when it is new or changed on disk, for what it is and the sha256 of its bytes, by which its notes are kept, and its thumbnail is made as a gallery take's is. Nothing of a round or a take is kept in the `.vgx`: a take whose report is gone leaves the strip at the next `takes`.

A click on a take shows it in the working area alone, as a take of the gallery is shown, `chosen` on the strip; anything the gallery does shows a take of its own again. While a round's take is shown, the grid button shows its round whole, as vg-frame-strips shows: each of its takes a row of frames at equal times, the time under each, as the gallery's strips are, with the round's line above them, "Round N · <model name> · N takes" (`layers::words::takes_word`), and each of its references by its order and role, "1st, start frame: the door." (`layers::words::sheet_label`), in a column at their left; the grid again shows the take. A Ctrl- or Shift-click (`film_select` with `extend`) on another take, of the same round, another, or the gallery, shows it beside the round's take shown, as vg-takes-compare shows; a round's take Ctrl-clicked while a gallery's take is shown goes beside that one, on the right. The two play from one time under one transport row, each a frame at that time at its own rate; each is captioned with its name, a round's "Take N · <model name>", and both are `chosen` on the strip. Showing a round whole or two takes together is a view of the working area (`rounds::With`): never a step of the gallery's history, and never kept in the `.vgx`, whose own view stays as it was; Edit stays the only way a round's take joins the gallery. Its menu holds, in vg-take-chosen's order, Next round from this (`take_next_round`), Use this take (`take_use`), Retime… (`take_retime`) and Upscale… (`take_upscale`), on only in a project, where the runner sends them, Edit (`take_edit`), Deliver… (`take_deliver`), for a finished take Compare with the source (`take_compare`), and Open report (`take_report`); each carries `take`, its path.

- **Next round from this** makes a draft of the take's round: its prompt, its references in order with their roles, and the take with the role Reference, with the current draft's settings or a new draft's. A current draft not yet run takes it in place; otherwise it is a new cell, shown. One step of the gallery's history.
- **Use this take** sends `use_take` with the tab, the cell's item as `takes` names it, and the take's path, the tab's file written first when it has none, beside the takes in use, replacing none, so it asks nothing first. The host answers `take_used`: an error shows its sentence as the failed notice, at the working area's bottom left; a success says nothing, as the seal, `badge-check` from `canon`, comes with the next `takes`.
- **Edit** adds the take to the gallery as a take of its own, linking its file, and shows it.
- **Open report** sends `open_report` with the take's round's request.

A project's files are filed, and nothing is declared as an item: no `items` is sent, `render` answers any item "That is not an item of this video.", and nothing is written under `.spumcake/renders/`.

Every project is organized one way, the way Last order is, so `project` is read for its folder alone, and a tab never written goes to the project's `work/` folder.

## Finishing

Retime… and Upscale… on a round's take, as vg-retime-upscale shows, each make a new draft cell of kind video, one step of the gallery's history, shown on the Prompt page, with no prompt and the take its one reference, a path with the role structure, as Spumcake-Skills' prompt roles name it, filling `video_url`, and the finishing endpoint's model and settings, as Spumcake-Skills' `generate-finished-shot` lists them and Last order's finishing requests sent them, fal's `enable_safety_checker` included: Retime's to 60 frames a second, Upscale's to 3840 wide. The endpoints and their settings are in `src/finishing.rs`, and only there. ## The `.vgx` file

A tab's gallery in a project is one `.vgx` file in the project's `work/` folder, written by itself about a second after a change, when the program is idle, as the Image Generator's `.ipx` files are, and before its tab closes, a `keep` is answered, or the host quits. With no project open, Save and Save As write it where the person chooses. Inside a project, the take shown and the frame are kept too, so they change the file; outside one, only the gallery's steps ask before a close. It holds no video, and no prompt, report, or record of what made a take: those are read from the project's own records.

Names follow the conventions guide, through `protocol::untitled` and `protocol::free_name`. A new tab is "Untitled Video", or the first free name after it among the open tabs and the `.vgx` files in the project's folder, and a tab never written goes to a file named the same way. A take is named after its file, free among the gallery's takes, and a copy is "<name> copy", numbered the same way when taken.

1. The five bytes `VGX1` and a newline.
2. One line of compact JSON, ended by a newline:
   - `version`: 3;
   - `shown`: the position of the take shown, from 0;
   - `takes`: every take, in filmstrip order, each with `name` and `link`: `path`, relative to the project's folder with `/` between folders when the file is inside it, or else absolute, and `sha256`, the file's in lowercase hex, as Spumcake-Skills records it;
   - `view`: `{"mode": "single"}`, `{"mode": "strips"}`, or `{"mode": "side", "with": n}`, side by side with the take at position `n`;
   - `frame`: the frame shown, from 0;
   - `cells`, from version 2, the tab's cells, each a step pointer or a draft as `layers::cells::stored_cell` keeps it, and `cell`, the one the Prompt page shows;
   - `take_notes`, from version 2, when any take has notes: each take's notes, with its `path` as the take's link names it and its `sha256`, and `notes`, `pins` and `marks` as the Image Generator's `.ipx` keeps them, each with `at`, its time in seconds;
   - `take_sound`, from version 3, when it is not at 0 dB and heard: the take's own sound, `gain_db` and `muted`, applying to whichever take is shown;
   - `sounds`, from version 3, when there are any: each sound layer, top first, with `name`, `link` as a take's, `gain_db`, `start` in seconds, and `muted`.

Version 2 is the same without `take_sound` and `sounds`, and version 1, milestone 1's file, without `cells`, `cell`, and `take_notes` too; both open as before.

A relative path is read from the project's folder, or, with no project, from the `.vgx` file's own. A take whose file is gone, or no longer reads as a video, keeps its place and its link and is drawn empty.
