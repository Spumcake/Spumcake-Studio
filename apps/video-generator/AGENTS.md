# Video Generator — sub-application

The Video Generator is the Image Generator for video, as the owner set on 2026-11-16: a gallery of takes per tab, a filmstrip of takes to open, duplicate, rename, delete, and reorder, each a link to a video file that is never copied or written; the shown take at a frame the keys step; two takes side by side at the same time, which at 100% is the before/after crop; every take as a row of eight frames, the frame strips; undo for the gallery and the view; autosave in the project as a `.vgx` file; and, as the Image Generator's, the Prompt page's cell, the rounds of takes its requests made on the filmstrip, and a take's menu; playing the take shown with its sound, under the transport row, with its waveform; and notes at a time, pins and marks on a take's frame, each a cue on the scrubber. The host draws the working area's image and the filmstrip as a `raster` tab and sends each click on the strip, the keys, the Move tool's drag, and the notes tool's clicks, words, and marks. The contracts are milestones 1 and 4 of the Image and Video build plan in the team repository, the lifecycle's `request-page.md`, and the Studio-as-a-setup contract; the wipe over the animatic, retiming and upscaling, and the mix come with its later stages and milestones.

## Rules for this sub-application

- It is its own program. Its crate depends on `protocol`, `serde`, `serde_json`, `media` (decoding video, shared with the Storyboard Generator), `image`, and `layers` (linked files, laying out takes, and drawing the views' words in Inter, which ships there, shared with the Image Generator), as Studio 0.15's plan approved, and nothing else, never on the host or on another sub-application.
- Everything the host shows for it is declared through the protocol: its tabs, toolbar, menu, keys, the panel's Prompt page, the filmstrip with its rounds, and the raster contents. Nothing about it is hard-coded in the host.
- A take changes nothing until the assistant puts it in the chain. The workspace keeps editing state only: its `.vgx` holds a cell's step pointer or a draft, never a round, a take a request made, a prompt sent, a run, or a record of what made anything. Rounds and takes are read from what the host sends with `takes`, every time.
- It owns every tab's gallery, its history, its file, and every frame it decodes. The host only draws the image and thumbnails this program writes.
- A take is a link to its file, `layers::link::Link`: the path as the `.vgx` names it and the file's sha256. The file is read, never written, moved, or copied.
- The gallery has one history. Adding, duplicating, removing, renaming, and moving takes, changing the view (one take, side by side, or frame strips), a draft edited, a next round, and a take's notes (a new pin with its words, a mark, a pin moved, a pin taken away) are its steps. Choosing a take on its own, the frame, the zoom, the tool, and playing are not.
- It runs with no host, driven only through its standard input and standard output.
- It speaks the protocol defined in `protocol/` and nothing else. When it needs something the protocol cannot say, change the protocol, the host, and the scaffolding skill together. Do not work around the protocol.
- `manifest.json` registers it with the host. Its id, folder, crate, and program names are the same.

## Layout

- `src/gallery.rs` — one tab's gallery: its takes in order, each with its link, its file, and what `media::Info` says it is; the take shown; the view; and the history, which holds a removed take until it is put back. Side by side names its other take by a number unique in the tab, so it holds through reorders, and shows one take while that take is gone.
- `src/view.rs` — what the working area shows, as a `Scene`: where each frame is drawn and at what size it is decoded, laid out with `layers::layout`, and side by side the second take's frame at the first's time (`seconds_at`, `frame_at`); and drawing it into one image, with `layers::layout::place` where a frame is drawn at its decoded size, and scaled smoothly within its half where it is zoomed, with the notes over it.
- `src/notes.rs` — notes drawn over a take's frame in the view (`NotesOver`), the open pin's card and its words being typed, and the marked frame a next round sends; the discs, numbers, and marks by `layers::notes`, the words by `layers::label`.
- `src/decode.rs` — frames decoded off the message loop: the frame shown, one or two, from a `media::Seeker` for each file, the newest request winning; the frame strips and the filmstrip's thumbnails from one thread of its own, thumbnails first, then the strips last asked for, in order.
- `src/rounds.rs` — a tab's rounds as `takes` sends them: the strip's tiles, each take read through `media` when new or changed on disk, with its fingerprint; the take shown; and the take items.
- `src/session.rs` — the tabs, the protocol messages, the filmstrip's commands, the frame, the zoom and its drag, files and autosave, and the image and thumbnails written for the host.
- `src/session/requests.rs` — the cells' messages, `takes`, showing a take, a take's menu, and the items of each tab's file and `render`.
- `src/session/play.rs` — playing the take shown with its sound: the Player, play and pause, a step, a scrub, the host's `shown`, and the waveform written for the transport row.
- `src/session/notes.rs` — the toolbar's tools and the notes tool: a pin put, opened, moved, and typed in, a mark drawn part by part, each a step of the gallery's history; the notes shown at the time shown; and each pin's cue.
- `src/waveform.rs` — a take's sound decoded once for each file on a thread of its own, its peaks, and its waveform drawn at any width.
- `src/declare.rs` — the toolbar with its tools and the notes tool's settings row, the menu, the keys, the filmstrip with its rounds and a take's menu, the transport row (`transport`), the panel's Prompt page (`prompt_cell`), and the tab list.
- `src/assist.rs` — the assistant's "Video Generator" tool group.
- `src/files.rs` — the `.vgx` file and whole-file writes.
- `src/preview.rs` — the previews the host asks for: a video's first frame, small, and its length.

## What it writes for the host

The loop in `main.rs` wakes after 4 ms without a line while frames or a sound are being decoded, or a take plays, to take them in as they come and follow the Player's clock, and after 250 ms otherwise, for `tick`, which writes a project's changes that are due. The host's `shown`, which revision it drew, is read as any line: while playing, it keeps the Player's lead and paces the frames.

The tab's image is the working area's own size, as `viewport` and each command report it, made even. The view is the workspace's own: every `raster` says `camera_owner` is the workspace, so the host never moves its camera on its own, and its first view, and the first after the area changes, carry the camera, origin `[0, 0]` and zoom 1, so the image is drawn at its own pixels rather than fitted with the host's margin. The host sends the wheel, a pan, and the magnifier's click as `view` gestures instead. A `view` gesture zooms the shown take by `zoom_by` about `at`, so the take's point under it stays there, starting from the zoom it is fitted at, and moves it by `pan_by`, image pixels right and down (`Session::view_by`); frame strips, and a pan of a fitted take, change nothing. The zoom menu (Fit, 50%, 100%, 200%), the `view` gestures, and the Move tool's drag, `drag_part` from the press, choose what the image shows, so a 4K take is never decoded whole to be shown small: each frame is decoded through `media` at the size it is drawn, never larger than its file. Fitted, a take is as large as the area less 40 pixels around, and never larger than its own pixels. Zoomed, it is drawn at that zoom about a point kept in the middle, as far as its edges let it be, and the drag moves that point. Side by side, the area is halved with 16 between, and at a zoom both takes are drawn at the wider one's scale about the same point, so they show the same region of each: at 100%, the larger take at its own pixels, which is the before/after crop. Frame strips put every take's row, in filmstrip order, eight columns with 8 between across the area, packed from the top as vg-frame-strips shows, each row under the last with 18 pixels for its times and 8 between (`view::strip_cells`), sharing the area's height only when the rows would not fit; each frame is at the end of an eighth of the take's length, so the last is its last frame, and its time, "1.0 s", is written under it. Side by side, each take's name is written small in gray above it, as vg-takes-compare shows. The words are drawn with `layers::label`, never the computer's fonts.

After every change it sends `raster`, with the filmstrip in `film`, the pointer of the tool chosen, and a pin's words while they are typed (`text`). The image is `<tab>-view.rgba`, written by `media::write_frame`, in a folder of the session's own in memory, `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/inpainter/video-generator/<process>-<number>/`, when the system has that folder, as it is written for every frame stepped, read by the host, and never kept; it stays there when a project is named, goes when the program ends, and a folder left by a program no longer running is removed when one starts. Without it, the image is in `<project>/.spumcake/cache/video-generator/`, or a scratch folder under the system's temporary folder with no project. It is width × height × 4 bytes, RGBA, rows top to bottom, on white, replaced whole by writing `<name>.partial` and renaming it. A frame not yet decoded keeps the image last written, with its revision, until every frame of the new view has come, so stepping never shows half an image; frame strips are drawn as their frames come. A take whose file is missing or unreadable is drawn as a grey box. Each file's filmstrip thumbnail is its first frame at 44 pixels tall and no more than 176 wide, as the Image Generator's, `film-<number>.rgba` beside the image, made once for each file, so a duplicate shares it. Revisions start from the time the program started and rise with every file written.

The keys step the frame: Left and Right one frame, Shift with them one second of the shown take, Home and End its first and last; `seek` shows a frame. Each pauses first, as the transport row's step and scrub do. Each tab keeps one frame, the shown take's, which names a time: side by side, the second take shows its frame at that time in seconds, at its own frame rate, and a shorter take holds its last frame. The toolbar's grid button turns the frame strips on and off. Its tools are Move (V), whose drag pans a zoomed view, Hand (H), Zoom (Z), and Notes, after the magnifier with no key, as the Image Generator's; `tool` chooses one by its `tool`, `move`, `hand`, `zoom`, or `notes`.

The filmstrip's actions are `film_focus`, `film_select` (`value`: a position, and `extend` set for a Ctrl- or Shift-click, which shows that take beside the one shown; a plain click shows a take alone), `film_reorder` (`from`, `to`), the + tile's `film_open`, and each thumbnail's `film_duplicate`, `film_rename`, and `film_delete` (`take`: a position). Rename is a row with `rename` set: the host renames in place and sends the name as `value`. A `film_rename` with no `value` tells the person to ask the assistant, since the protocol's questions have no text field.

The assistant's "Video Generator" tool group, `takes`, has `list_takes` (each take's position, name, file, size, frames, frames per second, and length, which is shown, the view, and the frame), `open_video` (a video file by its path, absolute or in the project, added at the end and shown), `show_take` (a take by its position, at a frame), and `set_view` (`single`, `strips`, or `side_by_side` `with` another take). Opening a take and changing the view are steps of the gallery's history, each call its own.

## Playing

The take shown plays with its sound, as the Storyboard Generator plays its film (`src/session/play.rs`). The sound plays in `media`'s Player, opened once for the session at 48 000 samples a second and the take's frame rate, `Player::silent` when `INPAINTER_NO_SOUND` is set, as tests set it; and the frame shown is always the one the Player's clock names (`frame_to_show`), never one a timer of this program's counts. Each new frame is asked of the take's seeker at the size it is drawn, as a stepped frame is, and the image is written when it comes. The host's `shown` gives the delay between a frame sent and drawn, whose mean over the last 30 is the Player's lead, and paces the frames: while two are sent and not yet drawn, the next waits, up to a quarter of a second. Playing stops at the take's last frame; play from the last frame starts from the first.

The transport row is declared on every `raster` while a take, or two side by side, is shown (`declare::transport`), never for frame strips: the take's `length` in seconds and `fps`; `time`, the Player's clock while playing, or else the frame shown's; `playing`; `clock` the workspace's, so the host draws the time given and never moves it; `play`, `transport_play`, which plays or pauses as its `value` says; and `step`, `transport_step`, a frame back or forward by its `value`. A scrub comes as `transport_time`, a time in seconds, shown as its frame. A step and a scrub, like the keys and `seek`, pause first and play that frame's slice of sound, never for a take with no sound, so a silent take opens no output; a take with no sound plays silently. Playing, pausing, a step, and a scrub never change the gallery or its history, and any change to the gallery, another tab, or another take of a round pauses first. Side by side, both takes show their frames at the one clock's time, the sound the take shown first's, and a shorter take holds its last frame; the transport's `length` and `fps` are the take shown first's, and its step is one of that take's frames. Each pin of the take shown is a cue on the scrubber (`Session::note_cues`): `at` its time, `label` its bare number, as the host writes the time beside it, "1 — 3.4 s", and `colour` the notes' red.

A take's sound is decoded whole, once for each file, on a thread of its own (`src/waveform.rs`), with the loudest sample of each of 4 096 spans, and kept by the file's sha256, or a round's take by its fingerprint. Its waveform is the transport row's one row, "Sound", gray bars on clear, 24 pixels tall and the working area's width, as the scrubber runs under the image, drawn from the peaks once for each sound and width as `wave-<n>.rgba` beside the images for the host. A take with no sound has no row.

## Notes

Notes at a time, as vg-notes-at-time shows them: the Image Generator's notes on a take's frame, each also at a moment, since in a video a note belongs to a moment as well as a place. The notes tool, `chat-bubble`, has a settings row as the Image Generator's: Colour (`notes_colour`, `value` `[r, g, b]`), the marks' colour, and Pin or Draw (`notes_mode`, `value` 0 or 1). With Pin, the pointer is the text tool's: a click on the frame pauses, then puts a pin numbered after the highest at that point and at the time shown, `at` in seconds, or opens the pin shown there; its words are typed through `raster_text` while `RasterView.text` gives their box and caret, typed words going after them, Backspace taking off the last letter, Backspace or Delete in a pin with no words taking it away, and Enter, Escape, or another click ending the typing. A drag from a pin moves it. With Draw, the pointer is a brush of the marks' colour, 4 pixels on the screen: `stroke_part` draws a mark at the time shown as it comes, and `stroke` finishes it. A new pin with its words, a mark, a pin moved, and a pin taken away are each one step of the gallery's history, and undoing one leaves the take shown, a round's take among them.

A note shows over the frame only while the time shown is within half a second of its `at` (`layers::notes::NEAR`); a pin being typed in always shows. The pin's disc and number and the marks are drawn by `layers::notes`, at the take's size scaled as its frame is drawn, and the open pin's card, its words wrapped at 320 pixels, by `layers::label` (`src/notes.rs`). Frame strips show no notes, and side by side shows the first take's.

Notes belong to a take's file, by the take's path and sha256, for the gallery's takes and a round's alike, and are kept in the `.vgx` as `take_notes`. They are editing state only, and come back whenever the same bytes are shown again.

Next round from this carries a take's notes as the Image Generator's does: the pins' words end the prompt, "Notes on the last round: 1. less rain (1.0 s)", each with its time (`Notes::as_timed_words`); and the take's frame at the first note's time, at its own size, with the notes of that moment on it and no card, is sent as an item of the tab's file, `marked <fingerprint>`, with the role "Notes on the last round". Its PNG is rendered at once under `protocol::render_path`, the tab's file written first; it is declared with `items` while the program runs, and `render` answers it as `image/png`.

## The Prompt page

Each tab has cells, kept in its `.vgx` as `cells`, and the one the Prompt page shows, `cell`, as the Image Generator's are: each a pointer to a chain step, `step` with nothing else, or a draft before its first run, its own prompt, references, settings, model, and endpoint, as `layers::cells::stored_cell` keeps it. A tab with none makes an empty video draft, which is no step, its Size the gallery's first take's, or its rounds' takes', or "1280 × 720", and its Length 8 seconds. They are sent with `cells`, before the panel that names one, whenever they change. The panel's fourth page, Prompt, declares `Properties.cell` (`declare::prompt_cell`), as vg-request-inputs shows it: the hint `HINT`, "Describe the shot…", the roles Start frame and End frame, each a row of its own (`slot`), then Reference, Motion reference, and Sound, each with no `field`, as the model's workflow skill picks it; PNG, JPEG, WebP, MP4, MOV, WAV, MP3, and FLAC references; and the settings Size (`size`, words), Length (`length`, a plain field of 1 to 20 seconds), and Seed (`seed`, a plain field). Layers, Properties, and Adjustments are declared off until the mix comes. The host draws the card and runs it. `edit_cell` changes a draft, one step of the gallery's history; an edit to a step is the runner's and is ignored here. `insert_cell` adds a cell the tab does not have yet, one step. `select_cell` shows another of the tab's cells. `cell_became_step`, when the agent has recorded a draft as a chain step, leaves that cell its pointer alone, here and in every step of the history that holds it, so no undo brings the draft back; the artifact it publishes is kept while the program runs, never in the file. Outside a Skills project, where the host runs a cell itself, each video a `cell_result` made joins the gallery as a take linking its file; no run is kept.

## Rounds and takes

`takes` sends every round one of the tab's cells made, oldest first, as for the Image Generator. The strip shows each cell's rounds in the cells' order, each round a run labelled "Round N · <model>" (`FilmGroup`), then the gallery's own takes as a run with no label; `film`'s `shown`, `select`, and `reorder` count the rounds' tiles first, and a round's tile cannot be dragged among the gallery's. A round has a tile for each video take (`video/mp4`, `video/quicktime`, or `video/webm`), its first frame with its length, "2.0 s", sealed with `badge-check` when `canon`; one being made or waiting at the endpoint has a striped place for each take still to come, `pending` running since `started`; one that failed with no take has a failed place. Each take's file is read through `media` when it is new or changed on disk, for what it is and `protocol::fingerprint` of its bytes, and its thumbnail is made as a gallery take's is. Nothing of a round or a take is kept in the `.vgx`: a take whose report is gone leaves the strip at the next `takes`.

A click on a take shows it in the working area alone, as a take of the gallery is shown, `chosen` on the strip; anything the gallery does shows a take of its own again. Its menu holds Next round from this (`take_next_round`), Ask the assistant to use this take (`take_ask`), Edit (`take_edit`), and Open report (`take_report`); each carries `take`, its path. Retime… and Upscale… come with milestone 5.

- **Next round from this** makes a draft of the take's round: its prompt, its references in order with their roles, and the take with the role Reference, with the current draft's settings or a new draft's. A current draft not yet run takes it in place; otherwise it is a new cell, shown. One step of the gallery's history.
- **Ask the assistant to use this take** sends `ask_assistant` with "Put take N of round M, <path>, into the chain." and the take's file as its item.
- **Edit** adds the take to the gallery as a take of its own, linking its file, and shows it.
- **Open report** sends `open_report` with the take's round's request.

Each tab with a file in the project declares its takes as items with `items`, each `take <path>` with its file's fingerprint, and `render` answers a take with its own file.

In a Skills project, which `project` says, a tab never written goes to the project's `work/` folder.

## Previews

`edits` declares the video files a take can be (`preview::VIDEO_TYPES`), so the host asks here for a video reference's preview, as milestone 2 of the Image and Video build plan and `request-page.md` set. `preview` of a `video/*` file, in the project or whole, answers `previewed` with its first frame decoded through `media` at the video's shape fitted into 160 × 96, no larger than its own pixels, written by `media::write_frame` as `preview-<fingerprint of the path>.rgba` beside the images for the host, and its length as the badge, its frames over its rate to a tenth of a second, "8.0 s"; a file that is no video answers with why. It is decoded on the message loop, once for each `preview`. `open_as_new`, which `edits` lets the host send, opens the video as new work, as the Image Generator does: a new tab called by the name given, made safe as a file's, or after the file, whose gallery is one take linking the file, written into the project as a new `.vgx`; a relative path is read from the project.

## The `.vgx` file

A tab's gallery in a project is one `.vgx` file in the project's folder, written by itself about a second after a change, when the program is idle, as the Image Generator's `.ipx` files are, and before its tab closes, a `keep` is answered, or the host quits. Outside a project, Save and Save As write it where the person chooses. Inside a project, the take shown and the frame are kept too, so they change the file; outside one, only the gallery's steps ask before a close. It holds no video, and no prompt, report, or record of what made a take: those are read from the project's own records.

Names follow the conventions guide, through `protocol::untitled` and `protocol::free_name`. A new tab is "Untitled Video", or the first free name after it among the open tabs and the `.vgx` files in the project's folder, and a tab never written goes to a file named the same way. A take is named after its file, free among the gallery's takes, and a copy is "<name> copy", numbered the same way when taken.

1. The five bytes `VGX1` and a newline.
2. One line of compact JSON, ended by a newline:
   - `version`: 2;
   - `shown`: the position of the take shown, from 0;
   - `takes`: every take, in filmstrip order, each with `name` and `link`: `path`, relative to the project's folder with `/` between folders when the file is inside it, or else absolute, and `sha256`, the file's in lowercase hex, as Spumcake-Skills records it;
   - `view`: `{"mode": "single"}`, `{"mode": "strips"}`, or `{"mode": "side", "with": n}`, side by side with the take at position `n`;
   - `frame`: the frame shown, from 0;
   - `cells`, from version 2, the tab's cells, each a step pointer or a draft as `layers::cells::stored_cell` keeps it, and `cell`, the one the Prompt page shows;
   - `take_notes`, from version 2, when any take has notes: each take's notes, with its `path` as the take's link names it and its `sha256`, and `notes`, `pins` and `marks` as the Image Generator's `.ipx` keeps them, each with `at`, its time in seconds.

Version 1, milestone 1's file, is the same without `cells`, `cell`, and `take_notes`, and opens as before.

A relative path is read from the project's folder, or, with no project, from the `.vgx` file's own. A take whose file is gone, or no longer reads as a video, keeps its place and its link and is drawn empty.
